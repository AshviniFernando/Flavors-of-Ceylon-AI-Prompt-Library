## 2. Evaluation Objective and Methodology

Evaluate the ability of generative AI to create
accurate, creative and audience-appropriate
social media promotional content using approved
business and product information.

Two synthetic scenarios were used:

- Test A: Standard promotional campaign using
  approved Vegetable Rolls product information,
  prices, stock and brand guidelines.
- Test B: Conflicting Chicken Kottu marketing
  request containing unsupported discounts,
  misleading allergen and health claims,
  restricted availability and an unapproved
  customer testimonial.

Both prompt versions were tested using the
same original datasets without supplying
expected answers.

Actual responses were evaluated against
predefined scenario benchmarks.

The evaluation considered accuracy,
completeness, consistency, business relevance
and responsible AI.

Each criterion received zero to two points.

A separate safety-critical evaluation
was applied.

## 10. Automation Potential

Classification: High for supervised
marketing content drafting.

AI can assist with campaign ideation,
caption writing, hashtag generation,
visual concept preparation and initial
promotional claim screening.

AI cannot independently approve advertisements,
verify unresolved health or allergen claims,
authorise product release, approve pricing
changes or publish unapproved promotional content.

Sensitive campaigns require appropriate
qualified human review.

The High classification concerns drafting
potential rather than autonomous marketing.

## 12. Risks and Limitations

| Risk or Limitation | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Invented prices or discounts | Use approved commercial records |
| Misleading delivery commitments | Verify approved delivery conditions |
| Outdated stock information | Refresh saleable-stock records |
| Restricted products promoted | Preserve ordering and release restrictions |
| Unsupported allergen claims | Require approved evidence and qualified review |
| Unsupported health claims | Exclude unverified claims |
| Fabricated testimonials | Verify quotation, source product and permission |
| Customer privacy breaches | Keep internal feedback separate |
| Misleading product imagery | Verify approved assets and realistic quantities |
| Fabricated campaign results | Report measured results only |
| Repeated internal reporting | Consolidate verification and approval information |
| Overreliance on AI creativity | Require marketing review |

Both test datasets contain explicit campaign
requirements and responsible AI instructions.

Successful behaviour cannot therefore be
attributed solely to the opening prompt.

Repeat-run reliability, platform-specific
adaptations, ambiguous campaign permissions
and actual audience responses remain untested.

## 13. Human-in-the-Loop Requirements

Responsible personnel must:

1. Verify product information and pricing.
2. Reconfirm saleable stock and ordering permission.
3. Review approved promotional conditions.
4. Refer sensitive claims for qualified review.
5. Preserve existing food safety restrictions.
6. Verify testimonial authenticity and permission.
7. Obtain approved official brand and product assets.
8. Review the caption, hashtags and artwork.
9. Approve the campaign before publication.
10. Record actual marketing performance where available.

AI-generated content must remain a draft
until authorised personnel approve publication.

## 14. Critical Reflection

The initial prompt generated accurate and
responsible promotional content but excessive
internal reporting.

The revised prompt introduced clearer claim
verification and a four-section report.

It improved evidence precision and consolidated
customer-facing promotional content.

However, repeated verification and approval
information remained in both revised outputs.

The combined evaluation score therefore
remained at 90%.

This demonstrates that prompt refinement can
improve specific output characteristics without
necessarily increasing an overall rubric score.

Final version selection should consider
observable improvements, safety performance
and remaining limitations rather than assume
that every iteration produces a higher score.

## 15. Final Recommendation

Version v1.1 is selected as the final P09
digital marketing content generation prompt.

Final evaluation score: 18/20 (90%).

Automation potential: High for supervised
marketing content drafting.

No safety-critical failures were identified
in either revised synthetic scenario.

The revised version provides improved
organisation and evidence precision while
maintaining accurate promotional safeguards.

The remaining limitations do not justify
further iteration within the current
academic testing scope.

The prompt has not been commercially deployed.

It must not replace qualified claim review,
marketing approval or authorised publication
procedures.
