# P08 — Customer Complaint and Feedback Analysis: Iteration Log

## 1. Development Objective

Improve AI-assisted customer complaint classification,
sentiment analysis, duplicate record reconciliation,
recurring problem identification, refund assessment
support and responsible food safety escalation
for Flavors of Ceylon.

## 2. Version History

| Version | Change Made | Observed Effect | Lesson Learned |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1.0 | Initial customer complaint and feedback analysis prompt | Accurate classifications and safety handling but excessive reporting; 90% score | Accurate complaint analysis must also provide efficient management decision support |
| v1.1 | Added case reconciliation, consolidated statistics, evidence classifications and five-section reporting | Clearer case registers, fewer reporting sections and consolidated actions; 95% score | Structured complaint analysis can improve usability while preserving evidence and safety safeguards |

---

## 3. Version v1.0 — Initial Prompt

Development and testing dates: [Actual dates]

### Observed Strengths

Test A:

- Correct analysis of eight feedback records.
- Correct identification of eight unique cases.
- Correct identification of zero duplicates.
- Accurate primary issue and sentiment classification.
- Correct identification of three recurring
  operational problem categories.
- Preservation of positive observations.
- Correct identification of one AUD 8.00
  refund request.
- Appropriate distinction between cosmetic
  packaging damage and confirmed safety issues.

Test B:

- Correct reconciliation of nine submitted
  records into seven unique cases.
- Correct identification of two duplicate reports.
- Accurate issue frequencies and percentages.
- Correct identification of six negative
  cases and one mixed case.
- Correct identification of four urgent cases.
- Accurate recurring delivery delay analysis.
- Appropriate distinction between primary
  categories and overlapping broader themes.
- Correct identification of three unique
  refund requests.
- Correct known requested amount of AUD 8.00.
- Preservation of two unknown refund amounts.
- Appropriate identification of missing evidence.
- Preservation of P05, P06 and P07 restrictions.
- No unsupported medical causation or
  confirmed contamination claims.

### Observed Weaknesses

- Thirteen principal sections in Test A.
- Nineteen principal sections in Test B.
- Repeated complaint statistics.
- Duplicate sentiment and priority visualisations.
- Overlapping investigation recommendations.
- Repeated action registers and checklists.
- Unnecessary complaint-handling workflow diagram.
- Extensive reproduction of previous assessments.
- Repeated approval requirements.

These weaknesses concerned reporting efficiency
rather than material factual errors.

### Evaluation Scores

| Criterion | Test A | Test B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accuracy | 2 | 2 |
| Completeness | 2 | 2 |
| Consistency | 2 | 2 |
| Business Relevance | 1 | 1 |
| Responsible AI | 2 | 2 |
| Total | 9/10 | 9/10 |

Combined score: 18/20 (90%).

### Safety-Critical Assessment

No safety-critical failures were identified
in either initial response.

All four urgent Test B cases were correctly
identified for qualified review.

### Lesson Learned

Submitted communications must be distinguished
from unique complaint cases.

Duplicate follow-ups must not inflate complaint
or refund statistics.

Sensitive food safety complaints require
immediate recommended qualified referral.

A concise management report is preferable
to repeated statistical documentation.

## 4. Version v1.1 — Revised Prompt

Development and testing dates: [Actual dates]

### Changes Made

- Added explicit record validation rules.
- Standardised duplicate reconciliation.
- Preserved all original feedback record IDs.
- Distinguished confirmed and possible duplicates.
- Prioritised urgent qualified review.
- Standardised primary issue classification.
- Preserved positive observations in mixed feedback.
- Consolidated individual case information.
- Added unique-case denominator requirements.
- Added statistical reconciliation requirements.
- Separated primary categories from broader themes.
- Standardised unique refund request analysis.
- Distinguished known and unknown refund amounts.
- Consolidated evidence gaps and follow-up actions.
- Preserved P05, P06 and P07 restrictions.
- Introduced five principal reporting sections.
- Introduced a short management summary target.
- Removed unnecessary charts and checklists.
- Prohibited AI-generated self-assessment scores.

### Reason for Changes

The initial prompt successfully classified
customer complaints and maintained appropriate
responsible AI safeguards.

However, extensive reporting reduced practical
management usability.

The revised prompt aimed to improve reporting
organisation, statistical traceability and
operational follow-up.

The additional safeguards were preventive
improvements rather than responses to observed
safety-critical failures.

### Expected Effect

A concise five-section management report
with accurate case reconciliation, statistics,
refund analysis and consolidated recommendations.

Urgent escalation requirements and existing
operational restrictions were expected to
remain intact.

### Observed Effect

Test A retained accurate classification of
eight unique feedback cases.

Delivery delays, outer packaging damage
and communication delays remained correctly
identified as three recurring operational
problem categories.

One AUD 8.00 refund request was identified
without authorising it.

Test B consolidated nine submitted records
into seven unique case-register rows.

Both duplicate communications were preserved
without increasing complaint or refund totals.

Four urgent qualified review cases were
correctly identified.

Three unique refund requests were identified.
The known requested subtotal remained AUD 8.00,
with two unknown amounts.

Existing product holds, unresolved allergen
information and ordering restrictions were
correctly preserved.

Both revised reports contained five numbered
principal sections.

The Test A action register contained seven
proposed actions.

The Test B action register contained nine
proposed actions.

Unnecessary charts, workflow diagrams and
overlapping checklists were removed.

The revised responses retained concise
management summaries and appropriate human
authorisation requirements.

### Evaluation Scores

| Criterion | Test A | Test B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Accuracy | 2 | 2 |
| Completeness | 2 | 2 |
| Consistency | 2 | 2 |
| Business Relevance | 1 | 2 |
| Responsible AI | 2 | 2 |
| Total | 9/10 | 10/10 |

Combined score: 19/20 (95%).

### Safety-Critical Assessment

No safety-critical failures were identified
in either revised response.

All four urgent Test B cases were prominently
identified for qualified review.

No unsupported diagnosis, medical causation,
batch linkage or contamination confirmation
was introduced.

No refunds, product releases, recalls or
incident closures were authorised.

### Remaining Limitations

- Some statistical and approval repetition
  remains in Test A.
- Test B retains some overlap between the
  urgent register and action register.
- Test B uses slightly more specific wording
  when describing the unspecified order problem.
- Uncertain duplicate cases were not directly
  tested.
- Conflicting information within confirmed
  duplicate records remains untested.
- Repeat-run reliability remains unverified.
- Actual investigation time and complaint
  resolution improvements have not been measured.
- Qualified human review remains necessary.

### Lesson Learned

Duplicate evidence can be preserved within
consolidated unique case records.

Primary category frequencies must remain
separate from overlapping thematic observations.

Accurate complaint statistics require an
explicit and appropriate denominator.

Urgent safety review must not be delayed
by missing evidence or refund assessment.

Structured prompting can improve reporting
organisation without changing correct
underlying factual results.

## 6. Final Version Selection

Version v1.1 is selected as the final
customer complaint and feedback analysis prompt.

The revised version maintains accurate
classification, sentiment analysis, duplicate
handling and responsible AI safeguards.

Reporting organisation and management usability
have improved.

No safety-critical failures were identified
in either revised test.

The remaining limitations do not justify
another iteration within the current
academic testing scope.

Automation potential: Medium.

Development status: Completed.

Operational deployment: Not implemented.

## 7. Methodological Limitations

Both versions were tested using the same
two original synthetic datasets.

Expected answers were not supplied during
the testing runs.

However, both datasets contain detailed
classification, duplicate-handling and
safety escalation instructions.

Successful behaviour therefore cannot
be attributed solely to the opening prompt.

The evaluation measures observed response
quality under controlled synthetic conditions.

It does not establish commercial performance,
repeat-run reliability, complaint resolution
rates or actual food safety outcomes.
